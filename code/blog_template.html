In the 1930s British towns built lidos the way they had once built town halls. From the 1970s they closed them by the score. Then, against the odds, some came back. I built an open register of every lido I could document and followed each one from opening day to today.

On 31 May 1935, Penzance opened a triangular sea-water pool on Battery Rocks, below the town, as part of the celebrations for George V's Silver Jubilee. By August almost {{published.jubilee_1935_visitors|int}} people had been through the turnstiles. Jubilee Pool closed in {{examples.jubilee.closed|raw}}, reopened in {{examples.jubilee.reopened|raw}}, was wrecked by storms in 2014, reopened again in 2016, and is now warmed by a geothermal well drilled into the rock beneath it.

That is the British lido story in one pool: a boom, a long decline and a stubborn comeback. Everybody knows the outline. Wikipedia says {{published.built_1930s|int}} lidos were built in the 1930s, that "hundreds" have closed and that {{published.open_england|int}} remain open in England. But there is no open list of lidos with dates that lets you check any of it. So I made one.

My register holds {{register.n|int}} lidos and open-air pools whose dates or fate I could document from open sources: English Wikipedia articles on lidos and on seaside towns and parks, Wikidata, and OpenStreetMap. Every entry carries a short quotation and a link to where it came from. On top of that I mapped every working open-air pool, lido and tidal pool that OpenStreetMap's volunteers have tagged.

One decade built most of them

Outdoor bathing places are older than the word lido. Cleveland Pools in Bath date from {{published.cleveland_built|raw}}, and Clifton's Victoria Baths in Bristol opened in {{examples.clifton.opened|raw}}. The word itself probably came into English through "Lansbury's Lido", the newspapers' nickname for the Serpentine bathing place in Hyde Park after it was revamped in 1930.

Then came the rush. Of the {{openings.n_dated|int}} pools in my register with a documented opening year or decade, {{openings.d1930s|int}} ({{openings.share_peak|pct0}}) opened in the 1930s. {{openings.y1935|Words}} opened in 1935 alone, among them Jubilee Pool, Tinside in Plymouth, Hilsea in Portsmouth and Sandford Parks in Cheltenham. London County Council built {{published.lcc_lidos|int}} lidos between 1920 and 1939. New Brighton's, opened in 1934, was the largest lido in Britain when it was built. Finchley Lido once took {{published.finchley_record|int}} swimmers in a single day.

Three decades of closures

The register has a closing date for {{closures.n_dated|int}} pools. {{closures.n_1970_1999|int}} of them closed between 1970 and 1999, and the 1990s were the worst decade, with {{closures.d1990s|int}}. The pools that closed had typically been open for {{closures.median_years_open|int}} years. The sources blame cheaper foreign holidays, council cost-cutting, ageing pumps and plain neglect; at Cleethorpes in 1978 and New Brighton in 1990 the sea did the job.

Put the dates together and you can count how many of the documented pools were open each year. The number peaked at {{curve.peak_open|int}} in {{curve.peak_year|raw}} and bottomed out at {{curve.low_open|int}} in {{curve.low_year|raw}}, with {{curve.max_shut|int}} standing empty.

That curve is, if anything, too kind. A pool demolished in 1975 rarely gets a Wikipedia article; a rescued, listed one nearly always does. The real decline was certainly deeper than my sample can show.

The comeback

This is the part I did not expect to be so clear. Of the {{fate.ever_closed|int}} pools in the register that closed at some point and whose fate I know, {{fate.closed_then_reopened|int}} have reopened. Only two of those comebacks happened before 2000: Brockwell and Jubilee Pool, both in 1994. The rest came after, and the typical pool was shut for {{reopenings.median_years_shut|int}} years first. Reading's Thames Lido, an Edwardian ladies' pool, closed in 1974 and reopened {{reopenings.max_years_shut|int}} years later.

Behind most of these are campaigns with names like Save a Lido Today, the Brockwell Lido Users and the Friends of Beccles Outdoor Pool. Many pools are now run by trusts, community companies or, at Penzance, about 1,400 local shareholders. It is not a finished story: Cleveland Pools closed again after floods in January 2024, and Brockwell's operator went into administration in April 2026.

Where you can still swim outdoors

Combining the register with OpenStreetMap, I can place {{combined.open|int}} working open-air pools, lidos and tidal pools in the UK. That is a floor, not a count: it finds {{combined.england_open|int}} in England against the {{published.open_england|int}} Wikipedia gives. But the pattern is plain. {{combined.share_south|pct0}} are in London, the South East and the South West, and {{combined.share_coastal_open|pct0}} are within 2 km of the sea.

The South West leads by a distance, with {{combined.sw_open|int}} pools, {{combined.top_per_million|f1}} per million people, and that is mostly the sea. OpenStreetMap tags {{osm.open_tidal|int}} working tidal and sea pools, basins on the shore that the tide refills, and {{osm.tidal_cornwall|int}} are in Cornwall: Bude, Chapel Rock at Perranporth, Millendreath, Porthtowan, Treyarnon and Trevone. The three northern English regions, by contrast, have {{combined.north_open|int}} working pools on my map between them.

How I did this

I searched {{wikipedia_articles|int}} English Wikipedia articles for lidos, open-air pools, bathing pools and tidal pools, read every mention, and wrote down each pool's opening, closing and reopening years with a quotation as evidence. Wikidata and OpenStreetMap tags added positions and a few dates. "Closed" means the closure after which a pool stood empty longest. Separately, I took every OpenStreetMap feature named or tagged as an open-air pool, lido or tidal pool, dropped the cafés, car parks, streets and hotel pools, and merged features within 400 m.

The big caveat: this is a documented sample, not a census. It holds only about {{thirties.share_of_169|pct0}} of the 1930s lidos said to have been built, and it leans towards survivors. Trust the timing (when pools opened and closed) more than the shares. If you know a lido that is missing, the data are open and I would love to add it.
